California medical cannabis, explained

Plain‑language guides to how access works in California — with links to the official sources behind each statement.

Recommendation vs. the county MMIC

Physician’s recommendation

  • Issued by a California‑licensed physician after an evaluation.
  • Says medical cannabis may be appropriate for you.
  • Not a conventional prescription; not a government ID.

The voluntary state MMIC

  • A voluntary card issued by your county program under the California Department of Public Health.
  • Helps law enforcement identify you as a protected medical patient, and can exempt qualifying medical cannabis purchases from state sales and use tax.
  • Costs up to $100 per application/renewal (50% off for Medi‑Cal); amounts vary by county. We do not issue it.

Sources: California Department of Public Health, MMIC Program FAQs and Roles & Responsibilities; CDTFA, medicinal cannabis sales & use tax exemption. Reviewed 2026-10-10.

Risks, side effects & interactions

Cannabis can impair driving, interact with medications, and isn’t right for everyone. See the balanced overview below.

A balanced word on risks

Good care means talking about downsides, not just benefits. Depending on the person and product, cannabis can:

  • Impair coordination, judgment, and driving — don’t drive or operate machinery while impaired.
  • Interact with prescription medications; tell your physician everything you take.
  • Cause side effects such as anxiety, dizziness, rapid heart rate, or dependence in some people.
  • Be inappropriate during pregnancy or breastfeeding, and for people with certain conditions.

If you have a serious or worsening medical problem, cannabis is not a substitute for appropriate medical care. When in doubt, talk with a physician.
